Beyond the classroom: competitions, challenges and certificates (Grades 6–8)

Checked on 9 November 2026 against each organiser's own website only, with one official source for each. Organisers change dates, so check the page again before registering. Academic Olympiads (Maths, Science, English, Computing) are on the Olympiad Plan.

Skill level and fit are our own judgement. Everything else comes from the organiser.

Brutally, before you pick

  • Many of these are city-based. The marathon is in Mumbai and the kids' triathlon is in Bengaluru. Only some are online or nationwide. Check the location before getting a Riser excited.
  • Team challenges need an adult coach. Robotics and STEM Racing need 2–3 Risers plus a coach for months. That's a staff commitment, not a weekend.
  • Match each one to a Riser's real interest (from the Passion Finder), not to collecting certificates. A Riser who loves building will get far more from one robotics season than from five multiple-choice tests.

Each one in detail

National Automobile Olympiad (ASDC with CBSE)

  • What it is: Introduces students to "automotive design, robotics, artificial intelligence (AI), mechatronics… electric vehicles (EVs), and sustainable mobility". It includes industry sessions with professionals.
  • Who: "Open for students of class 6 – 12 across all education boards… no age restriction." In English and Hindi.
  • How to enter: "Through their respective schools or individually" (self-registration on the Olympiad page).
  • Format: Qualifying round of "35 Multiple Choice Questions with a time frame of 35 minutes", then a regional round, then a final.
  • When: In 2026, qualifying ran April–June and the regional round August–September. Expect the same in 2027.
  • Skill level (our view): Easy to moderate. Curiosity about cars and technology matters more than school marks.
  • Official source: https://nao.asdc.org.in/

World Robot Olympiad (WRO) India

  • What it is: Robotics challenges on a new theme each year (2026: "Robots Meet Culture"). Teams choose one category: RoboMission (build and program a robot for set missions), Future Innovators (design a robot project on the theme), RoboSports or Future Engineers.
  • Who: "Teams of 2-3 Students in the same age group led by an adult coach." Future Innovators Junior: "students 11-15 years old (in season 2026: born years 2011-2015)".
  • How it runs: Future Innovators goes virtual round, then regional, then national. RoboMission, RoboSports and Future Engineers go straight to nationals. The 2026 nationals were 26–28 August in Hyderabad.
  • Skill level (our view): Moderate to hard. Future Innovators suits idea-and-presentation Risers; RoboMission needs building and coding.
  • Official source: https://wroindia.org/season-2026/

STEM Racing India (formerly F1 in Schools India)

  • What it is: "Students design, construct, and race miniature Formula 1 vehicles" using CAD/CAM tools. Endorsed by Formula 1.
  • Who: "Students aged 9-19", in teams.
  • When and how: Dates and fees aren't on the official page. Ask the organiser (STEMplify) how a non-school team enters.
  • Skill level (our view): Hard: design software, making and racing. A big project for a team that loves engineering.
  • Official source: https://www.stemplify.com/stem-racing-india/

Tata Mumbai Marathon: Dream Run

  • What it is: A 5.9 km run on Mumbai Marathon day.
  • Who: "12 years and above (born on or before 16.01.2015)" for the 2027 race.
  • When: Race day 17 January 2027. Registration (8–13 September 2026) is closed. Expect it around September 2027 for the 2028 race; places fill fast.
  • Skill level (our view): Achievable with a few months of steady training. A good first "big" physical challenge.
  • Official source: https://tatamumbaimarathon.procam.in/race-categories/dream-run/information

IRONKIDS Bengaluru (kids' triathlon)

  • What it is: "India's first triathlon experience designed exclusively for young athletes": running, aquathlon, duathlon and triathlon-style races.
  • Who: "Children aged 6 to 16."
  • When: 9 August 2026 in Bengaluru (done). Watch for 2027.
  • Skill level (our view): Moderate. Needs confident swimming for the water events.
  • Official source: https://www.canadianinternationalschool.com/ironkids-bengaluru

Chess: All India Chess Federation (AICF)

  • What it is: National championships by age group.
  • 2026 dates: Under-11 2–8 Aug (Mangalore), Under-13 3–9 Sep (Pondicherry), Under-15 12–20 Oct (Telangana). Expect similar months in 2027.
  • How to enter: Each event has its own circular on the AICF site. Players register with AICF ("New Player Registration").
  • Skill level (our view): Hard at national level. Local rated tournaments come first.
  • Official source: https://aicf.in/upcoming-nationals/

Cambridge A2 Key for Schools (English)

  • What it is: Shows "learners can communicate in simple situations". Tests reading, writing, listening and speaking.
  • Who: Made for school-age learners. It leads on to B1 Preliminary for Schools.
  • Format: Paper-based or digital (the adult A2 Key paper version ends after December 2026; the Schools version keeps both).
  • Skill level (our view): Basic English. B1 Preliminary for Schools suits stronger Grade 6–8 English users.
  • Official source: https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/key/

Trinity College London: music and drama grades

  • What it is: Graded exams and certificates in music, drama and combined arts, with "pathways for all ages, abilities and ambitions". Contemporary options let learners "play the music you love".
  • How: Through a Trinity centre or teacher. Fees and options vary by country (see the India page on the official site).
  • Skill level (our view): Grades go from beginner upwards. Choose the grade with the Riser's teacher.
  • Official source: https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/music

International Award for Young People (IAYP): Bronze

  • What it is: Part of the Duke of Edinburgh's Award. Four sections: a skill, physical recreation, service, and an adventurous journey.
  • Who: "Open to anyone aged 14 and above." Those not connected with a licensed institution can "join the Open Award Centre".
  • Skill level (our view): About commitment over months, not talent. It suits Risers turning 14, and fits passions well (the "skill" section can be their passion).
  • Official source: https://iayp.co.in/iayp-bronze-award/
