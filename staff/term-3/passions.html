The idea in one line

One level system for every field. A Riser moves up by showing what they can do. The app keeps track, reminds and suggests what's next; a mentor signs off each level-up. They can keep going as long as they want.

Not like Quests

Quests Passions
Team project, topic set for the term One Riser and what they love
Fixed weeks, deadlines, a build Their own pace; no deadlines below Level 4
Marked Never marked; they level up by showing
Ends with the term Keeps going for as long as they love it

The levels (the same for every field)

Names follow a "Rise" theme for Risers: a spark that grows into a light others follow.

Level Name What it looks like To move up, they show…
1 Spark Trying it out, often 5 feed posts over at least 3 weeks
2 Kindle Learning the basics from a teacher, course, book or videos One finished piece using what they learned, and what they learned from
3 Flame Making their own things, not just copying 3 original pieces, and one shown to someone outside LifeHub
4 Blaze Their passion project: something real, with an audience or a purpose A finished project, shared (an exhibition, a stall, a performance, a video, a website, a product)
5 Beacon Working with people in the field Feedback from an outside person they worked with or learned from (shadowing, a real job, a competition, a mentor)
6 Torchbearer Teaching others, leading, winning, selling Taught a workshop, led a team, placed in a competition or sold their work
7+ Star Going deeper for as long as they want A new, bigger goal agreed with their mentor, each time

Examples for one field (baking): Spark: bakes at home a few times. Kindle: follows a bread course. Flame: invents recipes. Blaze: runs a bake stall at a LifeHub event. Beacon: spends a day in a real bakery. Torchbearer: teaches younger Risers or takes orders. Star: enters a baking competition.

The passion project (Level 4: Blaze)

  • It only starts when they reach Level 4 and want it. No one is pushed into a project at Level 1.
  • They choose it, with their mentor: what they'll make, who it's for, and how they'll share it.
  • One page to plan it: goal, who it's for, three steps, when they'll share it. Not a task board.
  • It ends with sharing to a real audience, beyond LifeHub where possible.

Real-world experience (Level 5: Beacon)

Formal internships for 11–14-year-olds aren't realistic. This ladder is:

Step What it is Where it comes from
Watch Watch an expert at work (video, live event, exhibition) Online; Chennai events
Visit Visit a studio, workshop, kitchen, lab or office for an hour LifeHub parents' network, local businesses
Shadow Spend a day alongside someone who does it for a living Parents' network, LifeHub's contacts
Help Help on a real project as a volunteer (an event, a shop, an NGO, a performance) Local organisations, NGOs
Do A small real job for real people: a commission, a stall, a paid order from family friends Family network, LifeHub events
Compete Enter a competition or show in their field Beyond the Classroom
Mentor A regular mentor from the field (monthly call or visit) Parents' network, alumni, local experts

The most important piece: an Experts Directory. Ask every LifeHub family once: "What do you, or people you know, do for a living, and would they host a visit or a day's shadowing?" Tag each answer by field. That one list makes Level 5 possible for most Risers.

The Passion Feed (instead of a journal)

Kids see a journal as homework. So there is no journal: there's a Passion Feed. Each week, in passion hour, a Riser posts what they've been working on: a photo or a short video with a caption. Other Risers and staff react with 🔥 ❤️ 👏.

Problem with journals What the feed does instead
Writing for no one A real audience: other Risers and their mentor see it and react
Writing feels like schoolwork A photo or a video of up to 30 seconds, and a one-line caption (or a voice note)
A blank page: "what do I post?" A weekly question shown as a caption idea: "What went wrong today?", "What would you show a friend?"
Remembering to do it at home The last 5 minutes of passion hour is posting time, with the mentor. Done before they leave
Too much effort The mentor can post for them (a photo of what they're making)
Doesn't feel like it leads anywhere Every post fills their level bar
Nothing to show at the end At each level-up, a "then and now": their first post next to their latest
Others' work stays unseen Risers see each other's passions, which sparks new ones

Feed rules (to keep it kind and safe)

Rule Why
Reactions only: 🔥 ❤️ 👏. No comments (the mentor can add a private note) No mean or careless comments
Show who reacted, not counts. No "top posts" It must not become a popularity contest
Only LifeHub Risers and staff see the feed; each Riser chooses which posts their family sees Children's photos stay inside LifeHub
Staff can hide any post Anything inappropriate is gone at once
No streaks, no "you haven't posted" reminders to the Riser A passion mustn't turn into a chore. The mentor talks to a quiet Riser

Before we build it: private storage

Today everything the site saves goes to one store whose key is in the public code (see TODO.md). That's fine for quest progress, not for children's photos and videos. The feed needs private storage with proper sign-in first (for example a private file store with time-limited links, or Google Drive), and videos are too big for the current store anyway.

What the app does automatically

The app… How
Knows each Riser's passion From their Passion Finder answers (their ONE)
Runs the Passion Feed Photo or video posts with captions; reactions; family sharing by choice
Counts progress Fills their level bar ("4 of 5 posts")
Says what's next Shows the next level's steps, in their words
Lets them ask to level up A "Ready to level up" button that sends their evidence to their mentor
Records level-ups The mentor approves (or says "nearly: try this"); a badge appears
Tells the mentor Flags a Riser who hasn't posted in 2 weeks, so the mentor can talk to them (no nagging the Riser)
Finds opportunities Shows events from Beyond the Classroom and people from the Experts Directory in their field
Reports Their level and journey in the End-of-Term report
Shows staff One board: each Riser's passion, level, last post, and who's waiting for sign-off

What stays human: judging the work, approving level-ups, setting up visits and shadowing, and the conversations.
