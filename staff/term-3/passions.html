Passions: from first spark to real-world skill

Proposal, not built yet (apart from the Passion Finder). Decisions needed are at the bottom.

The idea in one line

One level system for every field. A Riser moves up by showing what they can do. The app keeps track, reminds and suggests what's next; a mentor signs off each level-up. They can keep going as long as they want.

Not like Quests

Quests Passions
Team project, topic set for the term One Riser and what they love
Fixed weeks, deadlines, a build Their own pace; no deadlines below Level 4
Marked Never marked; they level up by showing
Ends with the term Keeps going for as long as they love it

The levels (the same for every field)

Level Name What it looks like To move up, they show…
1 Explorer Trying it out, often 5 journal entries over at least 3 weeks
2 Learner Learning the basics from a teacher, course, book or videos One finished piece using what they learned, and what they learned from
3 Maker Making their own things, not just copying 3 original pieces, and one shown to someone outside LifeHub
4 Project Their passion project: something real, with an audience or a purpose A finished project, shared (an exhibition, a stall, a performance, a video, a website, a product)
5 Real world Working with people in the field Feedback from an outside person they worked with or learned from (shadowing, a real job, a competition, a mentor)
6 Leader Teaching others, leading, winning, selling Taught a workshop, led a team, placed in a competition or sold their work
7+ Master Going deeper for as long as they want A new, bigger goal agreed with their mentor, each time

Examples for one field (baking): 1 bakes at home a few times; 2 follows a bread course; 3 invents recipes; 4 runs a bake stall at a LifeHub event; 5 spends a day in a real bakery; 6 teaches younger Risers or takes orders; 7 enters a baking competition.

The passion project (Level 4)

  • It only starts when they reach Level 4 and want it. No one is pushed into a project at Level 1.
  • They choose it, with their mentor: what they'll make, who it's for, and how they'll share it.
  • One page to plan it: goal, who it's for, three steps, when they'll share it. Not a task board.
  • It ends with sharing to a real audience, beyond LifeHub where possible.

Real-world experience (Level 5)

Formal internships for 11–14-year-olds aren't realistic. This ladder is:

Step What it is Where it comes from
Watch Watch an expert at work (video, live event, exhibition) Online; Chennai events
Visit Visit a studio, workshop, kitchen, lab or office for an hour LifeHub parents' network, local businesses
Shadow Spend a day alongside someone who does it for a living Parents' network, LifeHub's contacts
Help Help on a real project as a volunteer (an event, a shop, an NGO, a performance) Local organisations, NGOs
Do A small real job for real people: a commission, a stall, a paid order from family friends Family network, LifeHub events
Compete Enter a competition or show in their field Beyond the Classroom
Mentor A regular mentor from the field (monthly call or visit) Parents' network, alumni, local experts

The most important piece: an Experts Directory. Ask every LifeHub family once: "What do you, or people you know, do for a living, and would they host a visit or a day's shadowing?" Tag each answer by field. That one list makes Level 5 possible for most Risers.

What the app does automatically

The app… How
Knows each Riser's passion From their Passion Finder answers (their ONE)
Keeps their journal Photos and short notes, any time, from their phone
Counts progress Shows what's done towards the next level ("4 of 5 journal entries")
Says what's next Shows the next level's steps, in their words
Lets them ask to level up A "Ready to level up" button that sends their evidence to their mentor
Records level-ups The mentor approves (or says "nearly: try this"); a badge appears
Nudges A reminder if there's no journal entry for 2 weeks
Finds opportunities Shows events from Beyond the Classroom and people from the Experts Directory in their field
Reports Their level and journey in the End-of-Term report
Shows staff One board: each Riser's passion, level, last entry, and who's waiting for sign-off

What stays human: judging the work, approving level-ups, setting up visits and shadowing, and the conversations.

What staff need to do

Who What How often
Mentor (a facilitator, or a parent or expert) Approve level-ups; a 10-minute chat with each Riser Monthly, plus whenever asked
One staff member Keep the Experts Directory and Beyond the Classroom up to date Monthly
Families Offer visits or shadowing; help with materials When they can

Decisions needed

  1. Time: how many hours a week of passion time, and when?
  2. Mentors: facilitators only, or parents and outside experts too? How many Risers per mentor?
  3. Experts Directory: can we ask every family for their contacts and fields?
  4. Levels: are the 7 levels and their "to move up" rules right?
  5. Rewards: badges only, or also a certificate and a line in the End-of-Term report?
  6. Safety: visits and shadowing need parent consent and an adult present. Who arranges them?
  7. Build order: suggested: journal and levels first, then the mentor sign-off and staff board, then the Experts Directory and opportunity matching.
