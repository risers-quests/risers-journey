Passions: finding them, and giving Risers time to flourish in them

Brainstorm, not built yet (apart from the Passion Finder).

The aim isn't another project to deliver. It's to find what each Riser truly loves and give them real time to spend on it, so it can grow into a skill at their own pace.

Not like Quests

Quests Passion time
A team project with a topic One Riser and what they love (friends can join in if they share it)
Approved by a facilitator Nothing to approve: it's their passion
Plan, build, a weekly Look Back No plan to deliver. They spend the time doing it
Task board, director, checkpoints No board, no roles, no deadlines
A build at the end, assessed Nothing marked. They grow at their own pace
A new quest each term Stay with a passion as long as it lasts, or change when it fades

How it could work

1. Find the passion

  • Passion Finder (built): what they love, lose track of time doing, and are curious about.
  • A friendly chat with a facilitator about their answers: "Tell me more about…". It isn't an interview to pick something.
  • Taster sessions for Risers who aren't sure yet: try a few things for a week or two each (clay, coding, cooking, music, photography…) and notice what pulls them back.
  • Watching for sparks: facilitators and parents note what a Riser keeps returning to by choice, in Quests, at home and at break.

2. Make time for it

  • Passion time that's protected in the timetable, for example two sessions a week, that nothing else takes over.
  • They spend it doing the thing: practising, making, reading, experimenting, getting it wrong and trying again.
  • Risers who share a passion can sit together if they want to. It isn't required.

3. Help it grow

  • Resources, not instructions: materials, tools, books, videos, a corner of the room. Facilitators ask what would help, then find it.
  • People who do it for real: a parent, a local artist, coder, baker or musician who visits now and then. Risers see what "really good" looks like and can ask questions.
  • The facilitator's role is to notice and nudge: "I saw you figured out…", "Have you seen how … does it?". They don't set tasks.

4. Notice the growth

  • A passion journal that belongs to the Riser: photos, sketches, short notes, videos, whenever they feel like it. Nothing compulsory, nothing marked.
  • "Then and now": every so often, put an early piece next to a recent one, so the Riser can see how far they've come.
  • Sharing when they want to: an informal sharing circle, showing family, teaching a younger Riser, entering a competition (see Olympiad Plan), or selling something they made. All optional.

5. Passions can change

  • Moving on from a passion is normal and fine. There's no "finish it first". A short chat helps them notice what they enjoyed and what they learned.
  • A Riser can have more than one passion, or none yet. "None yet" means more tasters, not pressure.

Questions to decide together

  1. How much time? How many passion sessions a week, and which slots? Does it carry on at home?
  2. Where does it happen? At LifeHub only, or partly at home with family support?
  3. Who helps? Facilitators only, or parents and outside people with the skill? How do we find and welcome them?
  4. Materials: a shared budget or a materials cupboard? What do families provide?
  5. Taster sessions: do we run a round of them first, before Risers choose?
  6. What staff record: a light note now and then (what the Riser is into, what they need) and nothing more? Should it appear in reports, and in what form?
  7. Sharing: a sharing circle once a term, or only when a Riser wants to?

What the app could do (later, and kept light)

  • Passion Finder (built). Staff read the answers on the Term 3 page.
  • My Passion page for each Riser: their passion journal (photos and notes) and "then and now" side by side. No tasks, deadlines or scores.
  • Staff view: each Riser's current passion(s), what would help them (materials, a person, time), and a short note now and then.
